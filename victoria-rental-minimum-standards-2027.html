Victoria's 2027 Rental Minimum Standards: the complete guide for property managers

A property manager's plain-English guide to every new Victorian rental minimum standard: when it applies, what it requires, and how rebates offset the cost across your Melbourne rent roll.

Updated 14 July 2026 · 6 min read · By JT Compliance

In short: Victoria's new rental minimum standards start 1 March 2027 (with draught proofing from 1 July 2027), adding ceiling insulation to R5.0, 4-star WELS showerheads, efficient electric heating, cooling and hot water, most triggered by a new lease or on end-of-life failure, plus one hard portfolio deadline for Melbourne and all Victorian landlords: efficient cooling in every rental's main living area by 1 July 2030.

Key facts

  • New energy-efficiency standards start 1 March 2027, with draught proofing from 1 July 2027, under the Residential Tenancies Amendment (Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards) Regulations 2025.
  • Ceiling insulation must be installed to R5.0 where none exists; if any insulation is already present, no upgrade is required regardless of rating.
  • Only one hard portfolio-wide deadline: fixed efficient electric cooling in the main living area of every Victorian rental by 1 July 2030.
  • Since 25 November 2025, rentals must meet the existing minimum standards before being advertised or when an offer to let is made.
  • The 2025-26 penalty unit is $203.51 (indexed by the Department of Treasury and Finance), and renters enforce standards through VCAT, with Consumer Affairs Victoria as the regulator.
  • Rebates offset the works: VEU discounts now, ceiling insulation up to $1,482, and Solar for Rentals up to $2,800 off (rebate plus matching loan).

Victoria's new rental minimum standards begin on 1 March 2027 (with draught proofing following on 1 July 2027), and they add a wave of energy-efficiency requirements to the 14 baseline standards that have applied since 29 March 2021. In short: ceiling insulation to R5.0 where none exists, 4-star WELS showerheads, efficient electric heating, cooling and hot water, and sealed draughts. Most of these are triggered by a new lease or by an appliance failing at end of life. There is only one hard, portfolio-wide deadline that applies to every rental regardless of tenancy: fixed efficient electric cooling in the main living area by 1 July 2030.

For property managers running whole rent rolls across Melbourne, the practical challenge is not any single upgrade. It is tracking dozens of properties, each with a different trigger date, and knowing what each one actually needs. This page is the pillar guide to all of it.

What are the 2027 rental minimum standards?

The new standards are set by the Residential Tenancies Amendment (Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards) Regulations 2025, which are finalised, not a proposal. They sit on top of the base Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, which introduced 14 minimum standards that have been in force since 29 March 2021. Consumer Affairs Victoria (CAV) regulates the standards, and renters enforce them through VCAT.

The full detail is published by Consumer Affairs Victoria and Energy Victoria.

Two nuances save property managers real money. First, if a property already has any ceiling insulation, no upgrade is needed regardless of its rating. Second, heating, hot water and cooling (before the 2030 deadline) are triggered by the appliance failing at end of life, so a working gas heater does not need to be ripped out on 1 March 2027. The one deadline that applies to every rental: cooling by 1 July 2030

The single hard, portfolio-wide date to circle is 1 July 2030. By then, every Victorian rental must have fixed, efficient electric cooling in the main living area, whether or not a new lease has started. From 1 March 2027 the same cooling standard is triggered on a new lease, so many homes across Melbourne will meet it well before 2030 simply through tenant turnover. The 2030 date is the backstop for the properties that have not turned over.

Everything else in the table is either lease-triggered or on-failure, which means there is no other "everyone must comply by X" cliff. That is why staged auditing works: you map each property's likely trigger and sequence the works.

What is already in force: advertising from 25 November 2025

One reform is already live. From 25 November 2025, a rental property must meet the existing minimum standards before it is advertised, and when an offer to let is made, not only at key handover. This was announced by the Premier of Victoria as part of a package of 150 rental reforms. For property managers, it moves the compliance check earlier in the leasing cycle: a listing that goes up before the property is compliant is now a problem, so pre-advertising audits matter across every Melbourne office.

The 14 existing minimum standards still apply

The new energy rules do not replace the baseline. Since 29 March 2021, every Victorian rental has had to meet 14 minimum standards, published by Consumer Affairs Victoria:

  • Structural soundness and weatherproofing
  • A fixed heater in the main living area (energy-efficient for agreements from 29 March 2023)
  • A kitchen with a sink and a 2-burner stovetop
  • A bathroom with a washbasin and a shower or bath
  • A toilet
  • A laundry, if one is provided
  • Hot water
  • Lighting
  • Ventilation
  • Electrical and gas safety
  • Window coverings for privacy in bedrooms and living areas
  • Windows that function
  • Smoke alarms
  • External door and window locks
  • Showerheads at 3-star (being lifted to 4-star from March 2027)

An enforceable breach can be taken to VCAT, and the 2025-26 penalty unit is $203.51, indexed by the Department of Treasury and Finance and published by the Department of Justice.

Rebates that offset the upgrades

The upgrades come with real incentives, and applying them changes the numbers on every quote.

Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU). Discounts are available now for heating, cooling, hot water, draught and weather sealing, and showerheads. For ceiling insulation, the VEU discount opens for general homes from 1 October 2026 (up to $1,482, with a minimum $200 customer contribution) and is expected to extend to rentals around 1 January 2027.

Solar for Rentals (Solar Victoria). A rebate of up to $1,400 plus a matching interest-free loan up to $1,400 (total up to $2,800 off, with the loan repaid over four years) is available through Solar Victoria. The renter household income cap drops from $210,000 to $150,000 from 1 July 2026, the property must be valued under $3 million, the address must not have been rebated before, and a maximum of two rental rebates apply per financial year. Solar PV is not a minimum standard, so this is an incentive only, not an obligation.

How to plan a compliant portfolio

The efficient way to handle this across a rent roll is to audit every property against both the 14 baseline standards and the six new ones, record each property's likely trigger date, quote with the rebates already applied, and keep documented evidence for each address. Ceiling insulation and showerheads are usually the quickest wins; heating, hot water and cooling can be sequenced against appliance age and lease dates.
