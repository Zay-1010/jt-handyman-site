What Real Estate Agents Need to Know Before Recommending Pre-Sale Repairs

Pre-sale repairs · 5 min read

Recommending pre-sale repairs to a vendor is a genuine balancing act — too little and the property doesn't present its best; too much, and the vendor's spent money that doesn't come back at settlement. Getting this call right is part of what separates a trusted agent from one who's just guessing.

What generally moves the needle at inspection

  • First-impression items — a tired front door, chipped paint near the entry, an overgrown or neglected garden bed. These are the things buyers notice before they've even stepped inside.
  • Small, visible defects — a cracked tile, a sticking door, a dripping tap — that make a buyer wonder what else hasn't been maintained, even when the issue itself is minor.
  • Fresh paint in key rooms, particularly where scuffs or dated colours are the main thing holding a space back.
  • General presentation repairs that make a property photograph and show well, since first impressions online often determine whether a buyer books an inspection at all.

Where over-capitalising becomes a real risk

Full renovations rarely return their cost dollar-for-dollar at sale, and vendors don't always realise this until it's too late. The repairs genuinely worth recommending are usually the smaller, high-visibility ones — not a full kitchen or bathroom overhaul, unless the property's condition genuinely demands it to be saleable at all. Knowing where that line sits is part of the value an experienced agent brings to a vendor conversation.

A useful question to ask yourself: would this repair change a buyer's first impression, or is it something only a building inspector would flag? The former is usually worth recommending; the latter often isn't, unless it's a genuine disclosure issue.

Why timing matters as much as the repair list

A pre-sale repair list is only useful if it can actually be completed before the campaign starts. A vendor who's told about needed repairs two weeks before photos are booked has very little room to act — which is exactly where a fast, coordinated repair team makes a genuine difference to whether the advice is actually actionable.

How JT supports agents with pre-sale repairs
